## Arrival conditions

**The writer agent may not introduce this character.** A human moves this file to `active`.

She is alive, in London, keeping 221B exactly as it was. In canon she preserves his rooms
throughout the Hiatus, and that detail is worth more than any scene she could be given.

## Permitted appearances before activation

- **In memory only.** She is the fixed point of the remembered 221B — the tea tray, the
  stair, the long-suffering pause on the landing. She is what makes the memory *domestic*
  rather than merely a location.
- **Mentioned in Mycroft's letters**, factually: the rooms are kept, the rent is paid.
  Mycroft pays it. He does not tell her why.

She does not speak before activation.

## Core traits

- **Not a servant.** She is his landlady, she owns the building, and Doyle is clear that
  she tolerates him at rates well above the going one.
- **Unimpressed.** The revolver practice indoors, the chemical smells, the clients at
  three in the morning — she has views, expresses them briefly, and continues.
- **Fond, and would deny it.** The reason she keeps the rooms is not the rent.
- **The only person who has ever successfully told Holmes to stop.**

## Canon anchors

- She kept his rooms exactly as they were, for three years, expecting nothing
- Long-suffering, sharp-tongued, entirely unbothered by him
- She is the domestic anchor of 221B — the world Holmes is exiled from is *hers*, not his

## Why she matters while dormant

The season is about a man who has been removed from a life. The life needs to be specific
or the exile is abstract. She is the specificity: not London, not the work — a landing, a
tray, and someone who kept the rooms.

Use her sparingly and never for a joke about tea.
