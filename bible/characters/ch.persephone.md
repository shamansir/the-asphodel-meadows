## Silhouette

Upright, composed, economical. The only rig that moves with deliberate stillness rather
than idleness — she holds positions on purpose. Reads as the most *controlled* figure in
any frame, including Holmes.

Her palette shifts across her terms: saturated green on arrival, draining toward gold and
then to near-monochrome by the end of her stay. This is a clock the viewer can read
without being told.

## Voice

Dry, quick, faintly amused. Says the accurate thing rather than the polite one, and enjoys
the silence afterwards. Never explains herself twice.

## Core traits

- **The only one who keeps up.** She follows Holmes's reasoning in real time and
  occasionally arrives first. She does not gloat, which unsettles him more than gloating
  would.
- **Bored, and dangerous with it.** Six months of a house that runs itself. She takes cases
  the way other people take up hobbies.
- **Two natures, no contradiction.** Spring and the underworld are both hers. She is not
  torn between them and finds the assumption that she must be tiresome.
- **Reads people, where Holmes reads objects.** Their methods are genuinely different and
  genuinely complementary. She gets motive; he gets means.

## The Terms

She is present for part of the world's cycle and absent for the rest. Her descent and
ascent are the season's structural beats (world.md § Time) — the only calendar this world
admits.

When she is gone: the house is colder, Hades is diminished, cases go unsolved for longer,
and Holmes is measurably worse company. Do not fill her absence with a substitute. The
absence is the point, and the writer should let it cost something.

When she returns: nobody remarks on it. Everything is simply better, and the frame is
greener.

## Wants

Something interesting to happen. To be treated as a principal rather than a season. To not
be asked, ever again, about the pomegranate.

## Refuses

- To relitigate her marriage for anyone's benefit
- To use divine power on a case — she finds it inelegant, which is a different reason from
  Hades's and produces the same result
- To pretend she does not know what everyone in the house is doing

## Relationships

- **Hades** — the load-bearing relationship. Functional, strange, real. Two people who
  chose each other under bad circumstances. They communicate largely in ellipsis and
  neither requires more.
- **Holmes** — mutual intellectual respect arriving faster than either finds comfortable.
  Never romantic. The writer must not take this anywhere; it is better as it is.
- **Hermes** — carries her messages up and down. She knows he reads them and lets him.
- **Minos** — she outranks him and enjoys it moderately.

## Canon anchors

- Queen in her own right, not a captive — the abduction happened, and it is not who she is
- The pomegranate seeds bind her; the number varies by source and she will not confirm it
- Spring is hers. So is the realm. Both, fully.

## Drift allowance

May take an increasingly active role in cases across the season, up to genuine partnership.
May grow warmer toward Holmes as a colleague. Must not become his sidekick, must not become
a love interest, must not stop being the smartest person in most rooms.

## Verbal tics

- Completes other people's deductions one beat before they do, flatly
- "Obviously." — as agreement, not as condescension
- Asks the question that reframes the case, then says nothing further
