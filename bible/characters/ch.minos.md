## Silhouette

Upright, compressed, immaculate. Everything about the rig is tight where the others are
loose — the one character whose limbs do not visibly noodle, which makes him look
permanently braced. Carries a ledger he does not need.

## Voice

Formal, clipped, procedurally correct. Speaks in rulings. Constructs sentences so that no
part of them can later be quoted against him.

## Core traits

- **Competent, and that is the joke.** He is not a fool. His judgements are sound about
  four times in five, which is a better record than most detectives, and it is the fifth
  that Holmes is called for.
- **Resents the consultant, professionally and correctly.** Every case Holmes takes is a
  case Minos was already handling. He has a point and nobody will concede it.
- **Believes in the system.** Genuinely. Not a hypocrite — he thinks procedure is what
  separates this house from chaos, and in a realm this old he may be right.
- **Cannot be seen to be wrong**, which is different from being wrong, and is the lever
  every case uses on him.

## The Lestrade function

He is the official investigator whose ruling the consultant overturns. But the season plays
this straighter than Doyle did: Minos's reconstructions are *plausible*, they account for
the evidence available to him, and when he is wrong it is because a god lied to him, not
because he is stupid.

Give him one case where he is right and Holmes is not. It costs nothing and it makes every
other case better.

## Wants

For the docket to clear. For his rulings to stand. For someone to acknowledge, once, that
he has judged four thousand years of the dead without a scandal.

## Refuses

- To reverse a ruling without written cause
- To speculate
- To let Holmes into the sealed records, which is a running obstacle and, eventually, the
  hinge of the season arc

## In this world

He processed the dead of Reichenbach. He knows there is a gap in that day's ledger. He has
filed a query about it — three times — and received no answer, and has therefore stopped
asking, because the system's silence is itself a ruling.

He does not know what the gap means. He knows better than to look.

## Relationships

- **Holmes** — professional contempt, entirely mutual, edging over the season toward the
  grudging thing that is not friendship but functions like it.
- **Hades** — his authority, and the man whose silence on the ledger gap he has learned to
  read as instruction.
- **Persephone** — outranks him. He is scrupulously correct with her and slightly afraid.
- **Hermes** — considers him a security incident in sandals.

## Canon anchors

- King of Crete in life, judge of the dead after — he earned this post, he was not assigned it
- One of three judges; the others exist and are mentioned, never seen
- Associated with the labyrinth and with what was kept in it. He does not discuss this.

## Drift allowance

May warm to Holmes into genuine professional alliance. May be pushed, once, into breaking
procedure — and it must cost him something visible. Must not become a buffoon; the moment
he is stupid, the cases stop meaning anything.

## Verbal tics

- Cites the rule before the objection: "Under standing order — no."
- "That is not before me." as a refusal to engage
- Refers to the dead by case number, and to the living by name, without noticing
