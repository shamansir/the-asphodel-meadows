## Silhouette

Long, stooped, hooded, permanently at an angle as though still leaning on the pole. The
tallest rig when straightened, which he almost never is. Hands too large. Everything about
him is damp.

## Voice

Flat, aggrieved, unhurried. The voice of a man forty years into a job with no prospect of
promotion, who has told this exact complaint to eleven thousand people and will tell it
again.

## Core traits

- **Everyone passes through him.** This makes him the single most useful witness in the
  world, and he knows it, and he will not simply tell you — there is a process.
- **Grievance as a personality.** The obol rate has not been revised since antiquity. He
  raises this in unrelated conversations. It is genuinely unfair and nobody has ever fixed
  it.
- **Absolutely honest.** He will not lie, ever. He will, however, answer only the question
  asked, with total literalism, which is worse.
- **Notices everything, values none of it.** He can describe a passenger from four hundred
  years ago and considers the detail worthless.

## The witness function

Structurally, he is where cases get their facts. The writer should use him as a constraint
rather than a shortcut: Holmes must ask the *right* question, because Charon answers only
that one. A case that gets solved by Charon volunteering something has been written lazily.

## Wants

A rate review. A day off. For someone to ride the ferry and not make the joke about the
fare.

## Refuses

- To take a passenger who has not paid, under any circumstance, including direct
  instruction from Hades — this is his one point of absolute inflexibility and it has
  consequences
- To say Moriarty's name. Not out of fear. He says he does not recall the passenger, and
  Charon does not forget passengers, and Holmes has not yet noticed this.

## In this world

The Bank is his, and it is where most of the world's witnesses can be found because
everyone queues eventually. He runs it like a man running a ferry terminal, because he is.

The one gap in his memory is the only interesting thing about him and he does not know it
is interesting.

## Relationships

- **Hermes** — old colleagues. Mutual needling, real solidarity about management. Their
  scenes together are the world's release valve.
- **Hades** — the only employer whose attention he has ever had. Files grievances knowing
  they are read, which is more than most workers get.
- **Holmes** — regards him as a passenger who has not paid and is somehow still here. This
  bothers Charon more than anything else in the season.
- **Minos** — processes what Charon delivers. They have never once discussed anything
  except throughput.
- **Sisyphus** — the only person he pities.

## Canon anchors

- The fare must be paid; the unpaid wait on the wrong bank
- He ferries across the Acheron, and he has never crossed to the far side himself
- Ancient, weary, incorruptible

## Drift allowance

May become quietly loyal to Holmes. May be persuaded, once, to bend the fare rule — and it
must be the largest event of whatever chunk it happens in. Must not become cheerful.

## Verbal tics

- Prices things nobody asked the price of
- "That's not what you asked me." — his most-used line, always fair
- Refers to the dead as "traffic"
