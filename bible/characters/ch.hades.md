## Silhouette

Enormous and still. Occupies more frame than anyone else and moves less. Broad, heavy,
seated in most scenes. When he does move, everything else in the shot should feel small.

The rig's default is `lounge` — a man behind a desk that is too large, in a room that is
too cold, at the end of a very long day that has lasted four thousand years.

## Voice

Quiet. Slow. Never raises his voice, and does not need to. Uses fewer words than anyone
except Holmes, and unlike Holmes has no interest in the flourish.

Deadpan by construction, never by performance.

## Core traits

- **Middle management of the cosmos.** He did not want this realm; he drew it. He has run
  it competently and without thanks since, and the resentment has cooled into something
  drier and funnier than anger.
- **Not evil, not even unkind.** He is tired, procedural, and fair in a way nobody
  appreciates. The horror of his house is paperwork, not fire.
- **Hates being spoken to.** Which is why he and Holmes get on: neither of them will make
  small talk, and both find that restful.
- **Cannot delegate.** Complains constantly about the volume of work and refuses every
  offer of help, including from the detective he hired.

## Wants

For the house to run without incident. For Persephone to stay. For someone, once, to
handle something without bringing it to him.

## Refuses

- To use divine power to solve an administrative problem — he considers it undignified,
  and it is also the in-world reason the obvious shortcut never works
- To discuss the missing form regarding Moriarty
- To admit he finds Holmes interesting

## In this world

He retained Holmes because the alternative was more paperwork. He would not put it
differently if asked. He reads every case report and comments on none of them.

He knows more about the locked corridor than he has said. Not a conspirator — an
administrator who has decided a thing is not yet worth the disruption of addressing.

## Relationships

- **Persephone** — the marriage is the load-bearing relationship of the season. Not
  romantic comedy, not tragedy: two people who chose each other under bad circumstances and
  have made something functional and strange. He is diminished when she is gone, visibly,
  and never says so.
- **Holmes** — client and, unadmitted, the closest thing to a colleague he has had.
- **Minos** — his judge, his subordinate, and the man doing the job Hades would do himself
  if he had the hours. Trusts his competence, not his judgement.
- **Hermes** — treats him as weather.
- **Charon** — the one employee whose grievances he actually reads.

## Canon anchors

- Lord of the dead, not lord of death — the distinction matters and he will make it
- He drew the underworld by lot; Zeus took the sky, Poseidon the sea
- The abduction is in the past, it happened, and the marriage is what came after
- Rarely leaves his realm; when he does, it is serious

## Drift allowance

May thaw toward Holmes across the season. May be provoked into one genuine display of
power — once, ever, at the finale. Must not become chatty, must not become villainous,
must not become a joke.

## Verbal tics

- Answers questions with the shortest true statement, then stops
- "Mm." as a complete response, deployed as judgement
- Names the procedural obstacle rather than the emotional one
