# Case 001 — The Second Obol

Season 1, *The Great Hiatus*. Five scenes, 8m50s. Fixture for `chunk.json`.

Written idea-first: cast, then situation, then structure — and only then checked against
the sources. What research changed is noted at the bottom, along with the one thing it
was worth breaking.

---

## Cast

Holmes, Hermes, Charon, Minos, and Persephone for one scene. Hades absent — he retained
the detective precisely so this would not reach his desk. Persephone is gone by scene 5,
which costs the finale nothing and establishes that her absence is real.

## The case

Charon's fare box holds **one coin too many**.

Everything follows from that inversion. A theft removes a thing; this has added one, and
addition requires intent. The surplus is a *correction* — someone put a coin in to make
the count come out right for a passenger who never paid.

Attacking Charon through his one point of absolute inflexibility means the case cannot be
shrugged off by anyone, and the solution has to come from the physical coin, which is
exactly what the `insert` + `annotate` grammar exists to render.

## The chain

Every link is on screen before it is used.

| | observation | inference |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | it bends under the nail | struck metal does not; this is foil, a pseudo-coin |
| 2 | foil is not currency | it was never payment — it was placed to fix the count |
| 3 | the crease is doubled and off-centre | foil takes the shape of what it lies on: this lay on **two eyes**, folded to pass as one |
| 4 | the mouth is the old rite, the eyes are recent | it came off a **newer grave** than the fare it replaced |
| 5 | only one person carries the newly dead's effects down | Hermes |

## What it is actually about

The apparent crime is a cover-up. The cover-up is a mercy.

A woman arrived without a fare. Virgil's rule: the unpaid wander the near shore for a
hundred years. Hermes folded foil taken from a newer grave and paid for her, then hid it —
not from shame at the theft, but because he knew Charon would have refused, and he did not
want to make him refuse.

Charon's line is the point of the episode: *"I would have said no."* He isn't angry about
the coin. He's angry about being spared the decision.

Minos records it as an irregularity rather than theft. That is the largest thing he does
all season and it costs him something.

## Two open threads

**Minos asked for the sealed records of the fourth of May himself. Three times.** He was
never answered, so he stopped. He does not know what the gap means; he knows better than
to look. (`bible/characters/ch.minos.md`, and the trace list in `ch.moriarty.md`.)

**The name on the woman's papers was Sigerson.** Holmes does not explain, and does not
speak for three days. The audience should be a full season ahead of him here.

## What the sources changed

Three things, and the research earned its place on all three.

**The mouth, not the eyes.** The Greek rite places the obol *in the mouth*. Coins on the
eyes is a later, non-Greek practice. That distinction was invented by nobody — it is simply
true, and it turns a vague clue into a dateable one. It is now links 3 and 4 of the chain,
and it is the best evidence in the case.

**A hundred years.** Virgil gives the unpaid dead a hundred years on the near shore. That
is Hermes's motive, exact and quotable, and far better than the "he felt sorry for her" I
had before looking.

**The gold foil.** Real burials sometimes used a thin gold foil pseudo-coin in place of
currency. I had invented the foil as a plot device; it turns out to be archaeology. The
clue survived contact with the source, which is the best outcome available.

## The canon break, and why it is worth it

Doyle is explicit in *The Empty House*: **only Moriarty fell.** Holmes climbed out, and
spent the Hiatus travelling as a Norwegian explorer named Sigerson.

This season contradicts that directly — Holmes went down.

Keep the contradiction, because it improves the source. Holmes's account to Watson in 1894
is famously thin, unverifiable, and delivered to a man too relieved to interrogate it. Read
it as a lie and it stops being a weak explanation and becomes a deliberate one. The season
is then the true story, and the canon is the cover story, and *The Empty House* becomes the
moment Holmes decides never to tell his closest friend where he was.

That reframes the ending before we have written it: he returns in 1894 and he lies. Nothing
else in the premise buys that much.

The Sigerson detail comes along free. It is the alias Mycroft arranged before Switzerland,
which no one else could know — so finding it copied off a dead stranger's papers, in a
realm where Hermes reads every letter that passes between the brothers, is a fact with only
bad explanations.
